# Glass Energy Systems References

## Reference Principle

Use high-end animated landing pages and MotionSites-style work as a quality bar, not as a template. The goal is a precise Glass-specific hard-tech brand surface, not a copied agency aesthetic.

Use high-end animated references for motion quality only. Do not copy their startup-hype tone. Glass should feel like hard-tech infrastructure, not entertainment, crypto, AI SaaS, or a founder portfolio.

## What To Learn From High-end Animated Sites

* One dominant first impression per viewport.
* A clear visual hierarchy before the copy is read.
* Motion that reveals relationships, not just elements.
* Strong imagery or system visuals instead of decorative panels.
* Sparse, confident copy.
* Large negative space with intentional density where technical proof matters.
* Microinteractions that feel polished but quiet.
* Responsive layouts that preserve the core idea at every breakpoint.

## Translation For Glass

* The hero should behave like a premium PCS conversion scene: hardware, power flow, and grid context.
* The architecture section should be the second major visual set piece and explain battery -> PCS -> grid.
* The design language should feel machined, technical, dark, and calm.
* Copy should speak to PCS suppliers, EPCs, integrators, C&I BESS developers, and strategic partners.
* Supplier trust should come from manuals, protocol maps, FAT readiness, fault-code review, warranty/spares, and India deployment readiness.
* The site should show disciplined focus and process maturity, not fake scale.
* The public story should feel commercially serious without exposing internal roadmap or future component plans.

## Full-Page Storytelling Reference Use

Use high-end references for full-page storytelling principles:

* Scene continuity from first viewport to footer.
* Scroll-led narrative progression.
* Motif repetition that clarifies system logic.
* Section transformation rather than disconnected blocks.
* A persistent object, rail, path, or trace that binds the page together.
* Cinematic pacing from top to bottom, with the architecture section as the technical peak.

Translate those principles into the Glass supplier-hardware-to-PCS-to-grid story. Do not copy reference subject matter, hype, gimmicks, gimmicky camera moves, or entertainment-site pacing.

## What Not To Copy

* Do not copy another site's layout, motion sequence, imagery, brand language, or component shapes directly.
* Do not copy generic Awwwards tropes when they reduce supplier trust.
* Do not copy SaaS dashboards, fake command centers, fake analytics, or inflated traction sections.
* Do not import large external UI systems unless a future task clearly requires them.
* Do not generate random mood imagery when the site needs a physical PCS/grid story.

## What To Avoid

* Generic Problem / Solution pitch-deck structure.
* Roadmap as a large public section.
* Decorative bento grids.
* Tiny uppercase labels repeated as section scaffolding.
* Fake customer logos or certification marks.
* Purple-blue AI gradient surfaces.
* Overly playful motion, bounce, or floating decorative objects.
* Important copy embedded only inside raster images.

## Signature Interaction Requirement

Every major redesign pass should include one signature interaction:

Battery -> PCS -> grid conversion flow.

This interaction should connect the hero and architecture story. It can appear as a controlled animated trace, a scroll-activated architecture sequence, or a direct manipulation detail, but it must remain readable as a static system under reduced motion.

For future 3D or near-3D versions of this interaction, use `3D_STORY_SYSTEM.md`, `3D_PERFORMANCE_RULES.md`, and `.agents/skills/glass-3d-story-director/SKILL.md`.

## Review Lens

Before implementation, describe the intended reference lane in one sentence:

"Premium hard-tech infrastructure site with restrained cinematic PCS imagery, precise power-flow motion, and supplier-ready technical copy."

If a proposed visual choice cannot fit that sentence, it should be removed or rewritten.
